ListingLens

From scattered housing listings to grounded, comparable decisions.

A retrieval-augmented application for renters and buyers to question individual properties, compare a shortlist, and trace every answer back to listing evidence.

Role
End-to-end builder
Focus
RAG, extraction, evaluation
Stage
Architecture & baseline
01

The problem

House hunting is a comparison problem disguised as a search problem.

Listing platforms help people discover properties. They are less useful once a person has a shortlist and needs to reason across inconsistent descriptions, hidden trade-offs, and dozens of attributes.

“Which of these homes gives me the best space for the total monthly cost?”
“What does this listing say about insulation, parking, and outdoor space?”
“What is missing or ambiguous enough that I should ask the agent?”
Product decision

Build a source-grounded research assistant for a user’s saved shortlist—not a generic real-estate recommendation engine.

02

The experience

Answers that show their work.

The interface separates extracted facts, listing-language evidence, and model synthesis. Users can inspect the source behind a claim and see when information is missing.

  • Ask about one property or a saved shortlist
  • Compare normalized costs and features
  • Open the exact listing evidence for each claim
  • Surface conflicts, missing fields, and stale data

03

System design

Structured facts meet passage-level retrieval.

Property questions mix exact comparisons with ambiguous language. The system uses a structured store for prices and attributes, plus hybrid retrieval over descriptions for evidence that does not fit neatly into a schema.

  1. 01

    Ingest & snapshot

    Save listing content with source URL, capture time, and a content fingerprint.

    Raw snapshot
  2. 02

    Extract & normalize

    Map price, area, rooms, amenities, and recurring costs into a common schema.

    Property record
  3. 03

    Index evidence

    Chunk descriptions by semantic section and build lexical plus vector indexes.

    Hybrid index
  4. 04

    Route & retrieve

    Send calculations to structured queries and open questions to passage retrieval.

    Evidence pack
  5. 05

    Generate & verify

    Draft the answer, attach field-level citations, and flag unsupported claims.

    Grounded answer
04

Evaluation

The demo is not done when the answer looks plausible.

The evaluation plan tests the full chain—from field extraction and evidence retrieval to citation faithfulness and comparison correctness.

Data

Attribute accuracy

Precision and recall for prices, areas, costs, amenities, and missing-value detection.

Retrieval

Evidence recall@k

Whether the answer-bearing listing section appears in the retrieved evidence set.

Generation

Citation faithfulness

Whether each claim is entailed by its cited listing field or passage.

Product

Comparison correctness

Accuracy on deterministic trade-offs, missing data, and conflicting listing claims.

Operations

Latency & cost

End-to-end response time and model cost by question type and shortlist size.

Safety

Abstention quality

How reliably the system says “not in the listing” instead of filling the gap.

Current status

Metrics will be published here only after a versioned evaluation set and reproducible baseline exist.

05

Build plan

Four milestones to a credible first release.

01

Current

Create a synthetic/public sample, define the listing schema, and benchmark lexical retrieval.

02

Next

Grounded property Q&A

Add hybrid retrieval, citations, freshness metadata, and explicit abstention behavior.

03

Then

Shortlist comparison

Route calculations through structured data and generate comparable trade-off summaries.

04

Release

Evaluation & deployment

Publish the evaluation set, failure analysis, monitored demo, and complete technical write-up.

Data responsibility

The first release will use synthetic, user-provided, or explicitly permitted listing data. Source terms, personal data, update frequency, and deletion behavior are product requirements—not cleanup tasks.
